Written for the grower, the ranch manager, and the field lead — the three people who have to keep the plants alive and have the numbers ready when someone upstairs asks for them.

Where this starts

Most of what gets sold to growers explains things you already know. This document skips that part.

You already have this

The budget and the actual

CropManage tells you what the crop needs. Your crew knows what ran and for how long. The water side of this is not broken and we are not going to lecture you about it.

Nobody has told you this

The reporting and the deadlines

What has to be filed, which agency wants it, in what units, by what date — and what it costs if it's late or short. That's the gap, and that's the whole document.

Where we're going

Part 1 — Short

Two minutes. Establishing what we're not going to spend your time on.

01 What you already have The recommendation and the applied water. Where they live today, and why that part is solid.

Part 2 — The part nobody explains

The reason this document exists. Read this part twice.

02 Does this even land on you? Whether you have to report at all depends on your basin, your agency, and how much you pump. Some ranches owe nothing. Find out which one you are before you do anything else.
03 Who wants what Your local agency and the state want different things, on different forms. The nitrogen program wants a third. Laid out side by side so you can see which ones are yours.
04 What's actually on the form Field by field. Per well, per month, and in acre-feet — not per field, not per season, not in hours.
05 The calendar Every date in the year on one page, and which ones apply to you. Including the one that catches everybody: the reporting year ends September 30, not December 31.
06 Hours don't count Your crew records hours. No agency accepts hours. What has to happen in between, and the two pieces of information most ranches don't have written down anywhere.
07 What it costs to be late, short, or wrong Fees, penalties, and the part people miss — that going over doesn't just cost money this year, it comes out of next year's water.

Part 3 — What to do about it

Practical. Things that can start this week.

08 Gather these six things The short list. Most of it is already in someone's head or on a whiteboard — it just isn't written down where a report can reach it.
09 What good looks like at year end What a finished, defensible record contains — so you can tell whether you have one before somebody asks.

Part 4 — Straight talk

The section other vendors don't include.

10 What we know, what we don't, and what we'll find out for you Which rules are settled statewide, which change from agency to agency, and the specific things we have not confirmed yet — named, not buried.
Before you read any further

Every number in this document either has a source or is marked as something we still have to confirm. Where the rules differ by agency — and they do, a lot — we say so instead of picking the version that sounds best.

You've been sold enough dry holes. If we don't know something about your basin yet, you'll read that in plain words.
